# The hardest game is the one people finish

## SLOP3733 · Week 6

```notes
Open with the oddity, not the answer. Ask the room who has played a Souls

```

---

## Start with something that does not add up

Souls games kill you constantly. They explain almost nothing. They take about
a hundred hours to finish.

By every rule about how you keep a player, they should have no audience at
all.

---

![bg right:40%](./assets/split-fighting-game.svg)

## But the people playing them gave up on easier games

A fighting game match lasts three minutes. You can play one badly and still
enjoy it. It asks far less of you to begin.

And plenty of people who quit fighting games go on to finish Souls games.

```notes
The panel is training mode: a dummy that never moves, a five-input string, a
three-frame window. Everything on it is precise, and none of it tells you
whether the combo was worth learning.
```

# Difficulty is not the answer

```notes
The ring has a seam in it. The dim arc is the attempt, the bright short arc
is the retry. Point at the seam — that gap is what the next three slides are
about.
```

---

## Why "it's hard" explains nothing

Two games can punish you exactly as much, and one of them empties the room
while the other one keeps people for a hundred hours.

So difficulty is not what is different between them. We need something we can
actually measure.

---

## Two numbers you can time with a stopwatch

- **Time to failure** — you start an attempt, and you find out it did not
  work.
- **Time to retry** — from that moment until you can try again.

Every activity has both of these. Reading a chapter has both. An assignment
has both.

---

![bg contain](./assets/fig-loop-comparison.svg)

## The three loops, drawn to scale

```notes
Say out loud that the axis is logarithmic and every gridline is ten times the
last, or the eye reads the gaps as equal and the figure understates itself by
four orders of magnitude.

The middle row is the one to dwell on: failing a combo is nearly free, and
the answer that matters is still two days away.
```

## How much can you learn in ninety seconds?

One thing. You cannot walk out of a ninety-second fight holding five
observations, because there was not time for five to happen.

That sounds like a limitation of the short loop. It is the part that works.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> It always sweeps after the second swing.

That is everything one attempt gave you. It is small, it cost ninety seconds,
and you can test it on the very next try.

---

![bg contain](./assets/fig-attempt-ladder.svg)

## One lesson per attempt, twenty times over

```notes
Point at attempts 3, 6, 9, 12, 14 and 16 — every one of them worse than the
attempt before. That is what makes the figure honest, and it is also why
people keep going: a bad attempt still hands you a dot.

The number in the corner surprises people. Twenty deaths at a boss is about
thirty-five minutes, not a whole evening.
```

# You never had to make yourself do it

Each time, you just wanted to know whether the next try would work

---

## Two things had to be true for that to happen

**You could name what killed you.** The game showed you the sweep. If you
cannot name the cause, there is no lesson to take into the next attempt.

**The boss never changed.** Between attempt 4 and attempt 20, the only thing
that moved was you — so an improvement was yours and you could feel it.

---

![bg right:40%](./assets/split-studying.svg)

## Neither one is true of your studying

Most people cannot say what went wrong in the last three hours. Nothing
showed you the sweep.

And the material is not the same thing twice: every time you come back to it
you are reading something slightly different from what you remember.

```notes
The page on the right is printed twice, slightly out of register, and the
clock has no hands. No legible cause, no fixed opponent, nothing measured.
```

# This week you play

## One boss. Twenty deaths. One written line each.

---

## What to bring back

Twenty lines, one per death, and each line has to be a claim about the fight
that could turn out to be wrong.

*It always sweeps after the second swing* is a claim: attempt twenty-one
either confirms it or it does not. *I was bad at it* is not a claim, because
no attempt settles it either way.

Bring the log to the Lab in week 7. There is nothing to do in that Lab with
an empty one.
