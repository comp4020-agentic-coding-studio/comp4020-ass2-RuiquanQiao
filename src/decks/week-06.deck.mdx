## A fact that ought to be strange

Souls games are the least accommodating mainstream games there are.

They kill you constantly, explain almost nothing, and take about a hundred
hours.

## And they sell to the people fighting games lost

A fighting game match is three minutes.

You can play one badly and still have fun.

Mechanically, it is the far easier thing to get into.

# Measure the loop instead

```notes

```

---

## Try to answer it without the word "difficulty"

Difficulty is what everybody notices, and it explains nothing.

Two games can be equally punishing and one of them empties the room.

---

## Two numbers, for any activity

- **time to failure** — from starting an attempt to finding out it didn't work
- **time to retry** — from that until you can try again

Neither is a judgement. Both are stopwatch facts, and you can measure them on
your own week.

# How big is the lesson?

```notes
Twenty-two grey lines and one gold one. The grey is three hours you cannot
summarise. Ask the room when they last finished a session able to say one
specific thing they now know.
```

---

## Ninety seconds holds exactly one thing

That is not a limitation. It is the mechanism.

A long session produces an impression. A short one produces a sentence.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> It always sweeps after the second swing.

Small. Cheap. Completely testable.

## Twenty attempts, twenty true sentences

```notes
Point at attempt 3, and at 6, 9, 12, 14, 16 — every one worse than the attempt
before it. That is what makes this figure honest, and it is the reason people
keep going: a bad attempt still hands you a dot.

The number bottom right is the one that surprises people. Twenty deaths at a
boss is thirty-five minutes, not an evening.
```

## Two conditions that make it work

**Legible tells.** You lost because of something you can name — which is what
makes a lesson extractable at all.

**The boss does not change.** Between attempt 4 and attempt 20, the only
variable that moved is you.

---

![bg right:40%](./assets/split-studying.svg)

## Most of your studying has neither

You cannot name why the last three hours failed.

And the material moves every time you look at it.

```notes
The page on the right is printed twice, slightly out of register, and the
clock has no hands. Both are deliberate: no legible tell, no fixed boss, and
nothing measured.
```

# This week you play

## One boss. Twenty deaths. One written line each.

---

## What to bring back

Twenty lines, one per death, each one a **testable claim about the fight** —
not about yourself.

*It always sweeps after the second swing* is a line. *I was bad at it* is not:
there is no attempt twenty-one that confirms or denies it.

The deaths are the data. Week 7 is the Lab, and you cannot do it with an empty
log.
