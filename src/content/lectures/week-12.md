Two things happen today. The exam, and the verdict on the thing you built.

## The exam

Set on the quarter you cut in week 10, from the material you nominated, and
written to test whether you can use the ideas rather than whether you saw them.

It is not comparable between students and it is not meant to be. Yours is harder
or easier than the person next to you depending on what you picked, which is why
it is not the score being marked — see the
[policies page](/policies/) for how that is handled and why we decided it that
way rather than forcing everyone onto one subject.

What it does establish, and nothing else can, is whether learning actually
happened. Every other artefact in this course is something you wrote about
yourself.

## Did the mechanism cause it?

This is the hard half, and the honest answers are more interesting than the
flattering ones.

Some of you will have a good result and be unable to attribute it. You picked a
subject you turned out to like; you had a light fortnight elsewhere; the
mechanism ran for four days and then you just worked. Say so. A frank account of
a confounded result is worth more here than a confident story, and confident
stories are easy to spot because they never mention the weeks that didn't fit.

The evidence you have is your logs: the verdict stream, the attempt lines, the
boss tree with its dates. Argue from them. Show us the stretch where the
mechanism was running and the stretch where it wasn't, and what the difference
looks like.

## What would you cut?

The last question, and the one that separates people who built a mechanism from
people who built a hobby.

Adding to a system is easy and feels like progress. Removing something is a
judgement: you have to decide that a part which cost you effort is not earning
it. Every mechanism worth keeping has a shorter version, and the person who can
name their shorter version understands their own design.

Come back to where we started. The failure loop was the only variable that
mattered. Of everything you built across twelve weeks, how much of it was
actually shortening the loop, and how much was you enjoying the machinery?
