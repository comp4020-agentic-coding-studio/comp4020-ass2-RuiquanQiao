For the last three weeks the mechanism stops being the assignment and becomes
the instrument. You point it at a subject you have no appetite for and see
whether it carries you.

## Picking the target

Two routes, and they are equally respectable.

**The Aversion Profile.** A short forced-choice instrument that puts you in a
subject you are unlikely to enjoy. It is not a personality test in any sense a
psychologist would defend, and we are not going to pretend otherwise — it is a
device for taking the choice away from you, which is its entire value. People
asked to nominate a subject they dislike reliably nominate one they secretly
suspect they would be good at.

**Your own nomination.** Better, if you are honest. The strongest version is a
subject you have already decided you need and have already failed to start —
that is the case the whole course is aimed at.

Either way the choice is locked this week. The mark does not move with how hard
the subject is, so there is nothing to gain by gaming it, and the person you
would be cheating is the one who has to sit the exam.

## Evidencing the dislike

One paragraph, submitted with Assessment 3, and it should be specific enough
that we could have predicted your behaviour from it. "I don't like maths" is
not evidence. "I have started the first chapter of this textbook three times
since March and never reached chapter two" is.

If you cannot produce a sentence like that, you have probably picked something
you are merely unfamiliar with, which is a different and much easier problem.

## Cutting a quarter

Thirty hours, roughly a quarter of a normal course, and that is a deliberate
ceiling — this course is twelve weeks and has no right to ask for more.

Cut it as a **coherent** quarter, not simply the first quarter. The opening
chapters of most subjects are the throat-clearing, and a quarter made only of
preliminaries will leave you with nothing you could be tested on. Find the
smallest slice that contains something the subject is actually *for*, and cut to
that, even if it means skipping material the textbook puts before it.

Then turn it into bosses, exactly as in week 8. Six to ten.

## The baseline

Write down what you already know before you start. This is not a formality: the
exam has to be able to detect movement, and without a baseline a good result is
indistinguishable from having picked something you half-knew.

Be generous to yourself here rather than modest. Overstating your baseline costs
you nothing and understating it will read, at the end, as the thing it is.
