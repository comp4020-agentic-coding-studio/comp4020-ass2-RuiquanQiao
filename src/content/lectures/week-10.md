For the last three weeks the mechanism stops being the assignment and becomes
the instrument. You point it at the thing you could not get through, and find
out whether it carries you.

## Not the thing you dislike. The thing you stalled on.

This is the distinction the whole assessment turns on, so I want to be exact
about it.

A subject you dislike is a cheap target. If the mechanism gets you through
thirty hours of something you find dull, what you have shown is that it makes
boring material tolerable. That is a small claim and not the one this course is
making.

The target is the course you **started, stopped, restarted and stopped again,
and still believe you need**. It is harder, and it is the only version of this
that proves anything, because it is the actual condition the course was built
for.

The trap people fall into is assuming they stall on things they don't want.
Look at what you have actually failed to advance on and it is almost never
that. It is the language you keep meaning to get back to, the maths under the
field you want to work in, the part of your own project you have been circling
for a year. **Wanting it was never the missing ingredient**, which is precisely
why willpower has not fixed it and why a mechanism might.

## Picking the target

**Route A — nominate your own.** The recommended one, and the one that tests
what this course claims. You need two things: a stall you can evidence, and a
reason the material matters that does not depend on this course.

**Route B — [the Draft](/draft/).** A short forced-choice instrument that assigns you a
subject you are unlikely to enjoy, for people who genuinely cannot name a stall.
It is not a psychometric instrument in any sense a psychologist would defend and
we are not going to pretend otherwise; its value is that it takes the choice
away from you.

Both routes are marked out of the same total. But the feedback on Route B will
say what it can and cannot show: it answers *does the mechanism survive
material I have no appetite for*, which is a real question and a different one
from *does the mechanism get me past a wall I have already failed at*. If you
can name a stall, name it.

Either way the choice is locked this week. The mark does not move with how hard
the subject is, so there is nothing to gain by gaming it.

## Evidencing the stall

One paragraph, submitted with Assessment 3, specific enough that we could have
predicted your behaviour from it.

> I have opened chapter three of this book four separate times since March and
> never once reached chapter four. The internship I am applying for lists it as
> a requirement.

That is two sentences and it does both jobs — the stall is dated and countable,
and the need is external to this course. Compare "I'm not very good at
statistics", which is a mood.

If you cannot produce the first sentence, you have probably picked something you
are merely unfamiliar with. Unfamiliar is a different and much easier problem:
nothing is blocking you, you just haven't started.

## Cutting a quarter

About thirty hours, and that is a deliberate ceiling — this course is twelve
weeks and has no right to ask for more.

Cut a **coherent** quarter, not simply the first one. The opening chapters of
most subjects are throat-clearing, and a quarter made only of preliminaries
leaves you with nothing you could be tested on. Find the smallest slice that
contains something the subject is actually *for*, and cut to that even if it
means skipping material the textbook puts before it.

One exception worth taking seriously: if your stall has always happened at the
same place, that place goes in the cut. Routing around it would make the whole
exercise pointless.

Then turn it into bosses, exactly as in week 8. Six to ten.

## The baseline

Write down what you already know before you start, and where you stopped last
time. Not a formality: the exam has to be able to detect movement, and without
a baseline a good result is indistinguishable from having picked something you
half-knew.

Be generous to yourself rather than modest. Overstating your baseline costs you
nothing and understating it will read, at the end, as the thing it is.
