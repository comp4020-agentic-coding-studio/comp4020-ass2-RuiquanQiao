This week you play, and the Lab is the playing. This hour is only about how to
come back with something usable, because attentive play is a different activity
from play and it does not happen by accident.

## You are not here to climb

The instinct in a ranked match is to evaluate yourself. Resist it. "I played
badly" is an opinion about you and it is worth nothing here. "From the first
back until the dragon spawned I had no idea what I was doing" is an observation
about the machine, and it is exactly what we want.

The distinction matters because self-evaluation is the thing you are already
good at and it has never helped you.

## The three notes

After every match, before the next queue, write three lines. Thirty seconds, not
three minutes — a protocol you have to psych yourself up for is a protocol you
will stop running by game four.

1. **The last moment I knew exactly what I should be doing.** Timestamp it
   roughly. What made it obvious?
2. **The longest stretch I had no goal.** Roughly how long, and what ended it?
3. **What told me I was winning.** Not whether you were — what *told* you.

By game eight a pattern shows up in line 2 that you would never have predicted,
and it is almost always the same pattern that shows up in your study.

## What counts

Eight matches minimum, and they have to be games where the result mattered to
you — ranked, or draft with people who are trying. A mode where nobody minds
losing does not produce the thing we are looking at.

If you have never played, play anyway. Being bad is not a problem for this
exercise; being bored is. Kelsen runs a queue in the Lab for people starting
from zero.

## Afterwards

Bring the notes to week 4. You will build the paper mechanism directly out of
line 2 — the dead stretches are the thing we are trying to design away.
