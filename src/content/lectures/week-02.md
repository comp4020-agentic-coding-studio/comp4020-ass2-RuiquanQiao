A ranked match is about thirty minutes and it is very hard to spend any of it
doing nothing. That is not because it is exciting. It is because three separate
clocks are running, and at any moment at least one of them is about to demand a
decision.

## Three clocks

**Laning** is the fast one. Minions arrive every thirty seconds; each one is
worth gold if you land the killing blow and nothing if you don't. So there is a
scored, checkable event roughly every two seconds, and a wave-level decision
every half minute: push, freeze, or trade. You find out whether you were right
almost immediately, and the cost of being wrong is small and legible.

**Roaming** is the medium one, and it is the interesting one, because it is an
explicit opportunity cost. Leaving your lane costs you a known quantity — the
gold and experience of the waves you miss — in exchange for an uncertain gain
somewhere else on the map. The game makes both sides of that trade visible. Very
little of ordinary life does.

**Objectives** are the slow one, and they are scheduled. Dragon and herald spawn
on a clock everybody can see, so the whole map converges on a contestable event
at a known time. The five minutes before a spawn have a shape *because* of the
spawn.

Between them, these three mean the answer to "what is the next ninety seconds
for" is never *nothing*.

## The layer above

The ladder is a separate machine and it is important not to confuse the two.

Displayed rank is tier and division — Iron through Challenger, four divisions
each up to Diamond, a hundred points per division. Underneath it there is a
hidden rating the client never shows you. When the hidden rating runs ahead of
your displayed rank you gain more per win than you lose per loss; when it lags,
the reverse. That gap is the entire reason your LP gains vary, and it is
deliberate: the display is a lagging, smoothed view of an estimate that moves
faster than it does.

Placements only gain. A bad opening cannot dig you a hole.

Demotion is gated: points bleed to zero within a division and stop there, and
only a further loss taken *at* zero drops you. Every drop costs one extra,
clearly signposted game.

## The thing to take away

**The match is where the fast feedback lives. The ladder is where the slow
reward lives. They are two systems bolted together, and they do different jobs.**

Hold onto that, because in week 9 we will find something the ladder cannot do at
all, no matter how well it is built.
