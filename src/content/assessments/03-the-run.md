> Point the thing you built at a subject you cannot stand, and find out whether
> it carries you.

Everything before this was rehearsal on material you had already chosen to care
about. This is the case the course exists for: a subject you need, do not enjoy,
and have already failed to start at least once.

## What you do

**Pick the target** in week 10 — by the Aversion Profile, or by nominating your
own. Locked once chosen.

**Cut a quarter.** About thirty hours. A coherent quarter, not merely the first
one: the smallest slice that contains something the subject is actually *for*,
even if that means skipping preliminaries the textbook puts first.

**Run it** for weeks 10 to 12, with both mechanisms wired together as in week
11 — bosses give the route, sittings give the work, and every sitting belongs
to exactly one named boss.

**Sit the exam** in week 12.

## The exam

Set by us on the quarter you nominated, from your own material, and written to
test whether you can use the ideas rather than whether you have seen them.

It is not comparable between students and it is not intended to be — yours is
harder or easier than your neighbour's depending on what you picked. That is
why it is 40% rather than everything, and why the criterion is *the exam*
rather than *the exam score*: what it establishes is that learning happened at
all, against the baseline you wrote in week 10. Every other artefact you hand
in is something you wrote about yourself, and this is the only one that isn't.
The reasoning behind marking it this way is on the [policies
page](/policies/), including what we considered and rejected.

## What you hand in

- **The logs.** The verdict stream, the boss tree, the attempt lines, with
  dates. Gaps stay in. A fortnight with a hole in it, shown, is worth more than
  a tidy record, and the sittings you didn't log are never a random sample.
- **The evidence of dislike** — one paragraph, specific enough that we could
  have predicted your behaviour from it.
- **The baseline** you wrote in week 10, unedited.
- **The account** — two pages. Did the mechanism cause the result?

## On the account

The confounded answers are the good ones. Some of you will do well and be unable
to attribute it: you picked something you turned out to like, or the mechanism
ran four days and then you simply worked. Say so, and show which stretch was
which. A frank account of a confounded result marks better than a confident
story, and confident stories are easy to spot because they never mention the
weeks that didn't fit.

Argue from the logs, not from memory. By week 12 your memory of week 10 has been
replaced by a story about week 10, and the logs are the only part of this that
did not get rewritten.
