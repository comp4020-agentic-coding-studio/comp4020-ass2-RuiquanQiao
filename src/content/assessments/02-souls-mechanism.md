> Take something large enough to frighten you and turn it into a list of things
> that each end.

[Assessment 1](/assessments/01-lol-mechanism/) bought you the inside of thirty
minutes. This one is the other half, and it is the half that decides whether a
hundred hours is survivable.

## Two routes, equally marked

**Simple** — a sheet per boss, a wall of cards, a notebook with one line per
attempt.

**Coded** — a boss tree that rolls up, an attempt log you can query, a view that
shows you what is open.

Again neither scores higher, and if you built Assessment 1 on paper this is a
good week to build in code, or the reverse. Doing both routes once across the
two assessments is worth more to you than doing your comfortable one twice.

## What it has to do

- **Bosses that are defeatable.** Specific, and with a kill condition written in
  advance that somebody else could check. Six to ten of them, not forty.
- **Tells.** For each boss, how you will recognise the failure when it happens —
  the shape of the wrongness, not the fact of it.
- **An attempt log.** One line per attempt, each naming something about the
  work rather than something about your character. *I was lazy* is not a line.
- **Roll-up.** A boss's remaining work includes its children's, or the tree
  flatters you.
- **No fixed durations.** Estimate the work and let the time fall out of it. A
  length you commit to in advance turns a campaign into a sentence.

## What you submit

Demonstrated live in the second half of the week 9 lecture, about six minutes:

1. The tree, and one boss walked through end to end — how it was named, what
   its kill condition is, what the attempt log says.
2. A boss that **changed**. Split into three, closed early, rewritten because
   the kill condition turned out to be uncheckable. A tree with no history of
   revision after two weeks of use is a tree you have not been reading.
3. What the mechanism told you that you would not otherwise have known.

## The common failure

Bosses that cannot die. Almost every unsuccessful submission has one obstacle
that stayed open the entire fortnight, and in nearly every case the problem was
not difficulty — it was a kill condition that could not be checked, so there was
no possible event that would have closed it. Rewrite the condition; do not grind
the fight.
