I bounced off fighting games three times before I ever finished a Souls game,
which by any reasonable measure is the harder object. That is the puzzle I was
hired to explain, and the answer is not that I got tougher.

A fighting game asks you to go into a practice mode and drill a sequence whose
payoff arrives several sessions later, on faith. A Souls boss kills you ninety
seconds in and puts you back at the door in fifteen. You come out of each
attempt holding one thing — *it always sweeps after the second swing* — and you
want to know right now whether that one thing is enough. It usually isn't. So
you learn a second thing.

Nobody is being heroic here. The loop is just short enough that curiosity
carries you, and curiosity is cheaper than discipline.

The other half of what I teach is the part people miss. Before you start, a
Souls game looks like an unmanageable amount of content. You finish it anyway,
because it is a sequence of bosses and each one, individually, is a thing that
ends. That is a planning skill, and it is the one the ranked ladder cannot give
you no matter how well you build it.
