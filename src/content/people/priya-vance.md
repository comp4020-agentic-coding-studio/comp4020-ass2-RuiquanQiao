I ran three systems on myself before this course existed, and all three failed.

The first replaced time with a unit I called *effort* — one point for every hard
idea I got through — on the theory that an hour means different things on
different days but a hard idea always costs the same. It worked for a month.
Then the currency inflated: things that were not worth a point in March were
worth one by June, my daily totals kept rising, and nothing about my actual
output changed. I stopped counting.

The second was decomposition. I got good at breaking work apart, seeing what
depended on what, arranging it. It genuinely helped me think. It did not make me
do anything, and I can find weeks in that period where the plan is immaculate
and the record of work is empty.

The third was timer discipline. For nine months I logged every sitting. The
number that ended it: sixteen waking hours a day, of which six were at the desk,
on a good day. I had suspected four. Knowing it was six changed nothing, because
a timer measures how long you sat there and says nothing about whether anything
came out.

What all three have in common is that they measured what I put in. I was
recording my inputs and quietly refusing to look at my outputs, because inputs
are the part you can always feel good about. That is the mistake this course is
built around not repeating.

I still study badly some weeks. The mechanism is supposed to survive that, which
is why nothing in this course punishes you for a bad week.
